# John Company: the rules as they will be played here

John Company: Second Edition (Cole Wehrle, Wehrlegig Games, second printing 2023), 3 to 6 families, or 1 to 2 against the Crown.
Written from the rulebook in the print-and-play folder the owner shared on 2026-11-27 ("Rules of Play", 48 pages). The rulebook isn't
in this repository; page numbers below are its. The game is licensed under Creative Commons BY-NC-SA 4.0 (rulebook, page 48).

Nothing is built yet. This file is the rules in short, the readings where the rulebook leaves room (marked **Reading**) and the
questions it doesn't answer (marked **Open**). Card texts (offices, laws, prestige, blackmail, spouses, failure cards, events) are on the
cards, not in the rulebook, and will be transcribed from the print-and-play files. See `HANDOFF.md` for the plan.

## Components (p.2-3)

- The board: five prize boxes (left), the Company's tracks and boxes (middle), the map of India (right), and the red ribbon that
  orders the phases and the offices' operations.
- Per family (6): 18 family members, 1 VP marker, 5 promise cards, 1 opportunity marker, a family board, 2 firm cubes, 4 firm
  strategy cards.
- India: 20 tower levels, 8 domes (one per region), 3 sets of flags (empires), 20 event tiles (the Ganjifa tiles), 8 control/loot
  tokens, the Elephant (the looming Crisis).
- Pawns: red for Company Standing, Balance and Debt; black for the turn and the votes; silver for the phase. 30 orange cubes (fatigue
  on office cards, unrest on Company regions). 12 red Crown promise cubes (solo and two-player).
- Money: £1 (40), £2 (20), £5 (20), £10 (10); the bank is unlimited.
- Cards: 58 small enterprises (22 shipyards = shipping, 20 workshops = manufacturing, 16 luxuries = social), 17 office cards, 20 prestige
  and 10 blackmail cards, 26 law cards (Deregulation / Debtor's Prison only in some scenarios), 6 Company failure cards, 3 scenario cards
  with 18 setup cards each, 10 Crown AI cards and the Crown board.
- Ships: 22 player-owned (each matches a shipyard); 14 more, extra ships (one-turn leases) on one side, Company ships on the other.
- 20 regiments, 7 local alliances, 17 closed/filled order tokens, 4 power tokens, 8 Governor overlays, the Superintendent of Trade in
  China and Governor General overlays, 6 Presidency dividers, the Chairman and Opposition Leader markers, the Company share VP token,
  18 trophy/passed law tokens, 10 dice and the storm die.

## Setup (p.4-5)

1. Shuffle the India event tiles; place the local alliances in their Army boxes; Company Balance on 5; power tokens on their spaces;
   VP markers on 0; tower levels, domes and flags by the map; loot tokens in their regions, £ side up.
2. Pick a scenario: **1710** (play it first), **1758** (the Company starts with its monopoly, which can be broken), **1813** (no
   monopoly, private firms from the start; best with 5 or 6), **Long 1710** (as 1710 with Deregulation available, up to 8 turns, about
   twice as long). Follow its setup card: Standing, Debt, Company treasuries, passed laws, regiments, ships, India's regions, the turn
   marker and the Prime Minister dial's date.
3. Shuffle that scenario's player setup cards (plus 3 extra with 5 players, 6 with 6) and deal them evenly. Each player takes what
   theirs list: offices (a family member on the office, its card), cash, writers, officers-in-training, Commanders (on their Army slot),
   workshops, shipyards (the ship in the sea zone shown, or on the shipyard if unfitted), luxuries (+2 VP each), Company shares (a family
   member in the Court of Directors).
   - **Draft variant:** deal the cards, then pick one and pass the rest left until none remain.
4. The remaining office cards near the board. The London Season Display: prestige and blackmail cards shuffled together, 3 dealt
   (prestige face up, blackmail face down). The law deck shuffled.

## Key ideas (p.5-8)

- Components are limited by what's in the box (do as much as possible), except cash, trophy/law tokens, dice and regiments; the Company
  Balance, VP and Vote tracks are unlimited.
- **Everything is public except face-down blackmail cards** (discard piles too).
- Cash has a location: a family treasury spends on family actions, retirements, upkeep, taxes and negotiation; an office's treasury
  only on that office's actions.
- **Presidencies** (Bombay, Madras, Bengal) are offices with their own Army, writers, sea zone (West, South and East Indian Ocean) and,
  later, Governors.
- **India**: 8 regions, each with a tower (its strength; the dome isn't a level), orders (circles joined by dotted lines, each worth
  £; heavy-outlined ones are home ports), closed orders (covered), and an alignment: sovereign (big flag, or a dome with no flag) regions
  invade; dominated (small flag, or no dome) ones rebel. An **empire** is a capital (starred flag) and at least one dominated region; it
  adds its regions' strengths in attack and defence, and ends when only the capital is left.
- **The Elephant** marks the looming Crisis: on a border, the tail's region will invade the head's; in the centre of a region, or facing
  its sovereign, a Rebellion.

### Negotiation (p.8)

Players may transfer: enterprises, face-down blackmail cards (others may peek; never traded face up), cash from their family treasury
(to other families' treasuries only), shares (the receiver swaps in a family member of their own; never the Chairman's), and promise
cards (to anyone, back to their guarantor too). Not transferable: officeholders, writers, officers, pieces on prizes, spouses, passed
laws, played blackmail cards, the Prime Minister dial, trophies.

- **Immediate transfers are binding; everything else is non-binding.**
- **Promise cards:** each family has five, each with its promise on its back. A holder may "call in" a promise and the guarantor must
  fulfil it if they can (not during Final Scoring). Several called at once that can't all be met: the guarantor picks.
- **Golden rule:** a transferred thing can't activate twice (a workshop sold during Bonuses doesn't pay again).

## Sequence of play (p.10)

Each game turn: I. London Season, II. Family, III. Hiring, IV. Company Operation, V. Bonuses, VI. Revenue, VII. Events in India,
VIII. Parliament Meets, IX. Upkeep and Refresh. (In 1758 and Long 1710, a special session on Deregulation may come before I.)

### I. The London Season (p.10-12; skipped on the first turn)

1. **Attrition**, all players at once: a die per office card held, +1 per fatigue on it, +1 for the Chairman. 1-2 nothing; 3-4 add a
   fatigue; 5+ the officeholder retires to the Pensioners box, the office card goes vacant (face down in Vacant Offices), its fatigue
   back to the supply.
2. **Retirements**, from the Chairman (or former Chairman) clockwise: each pensioner may go to a prize box, paying its cost and gaining
   its VP. The money is set aside (it sets the order below) and paid to the bank at the end of the Season. Prizes have upkeep (phase IX)
   and many have window tax icons (phase VIII).
3. **Prestige cards**: from the player who spent most on retirements this turn (ties: most window icons, this turn's retirements
   included, then Prime Minister order), each player who retired at least one pensioner must take or discard one card of the Display
   (peeking at its blackmail cards first). Then discard the rest and refill to 3 (even if none were taken). Pensioners go back to supply.
   - **Spouses**: VP, often a restriction for the rest of the game, usually a retirement discount (stackable, own use, once per Season
     and once at Final Scoring); never transferable. Trades a spouse forces happen right after it is taken.
   - **Prestige enterprises**: special powers, count as enterprises of their type (power, policies), transferable.
   - **Blackmail**: a one-time effect, then face up in the player's area and no longer transferable; every blackmail card (up or down)
     counts for power at the end.

### II. Family (p.13-14)

1. **Family action**, from the Chairman clockwise: one action, marked with the opportunity marker; an action where the marker already
   was is taken twice. Laws may add one extra action (the latest passed only; no double, the marker doesn't move). With no family pieces,
   a piece is taken from a prize, losing that prize's VP. The actions:
   - **Enlist Writer**: a family member in one of the three Writers boxes; one more this turn if 4+ offices are vacant.
   - **Enlist Officer**: a family member in Officers-in-Training.
   - **Purchase Luxury**: £4; +2 VP while owned; a window tax icon; a social enterprise.
   - **Purchase Shipyard**: £2; its ship on it; £1 bonus a turn while its ship is fitted; 1 vote; a shipping enterprise.
   - **Purchase Workshop**: £5; £1 bonus a turn; 2 votes; 1 VP at the end if the Company fails; a manufacturing enterprise.
   - **Seek Share**: a family member on an empty Stock Exchange space, paying its price.
2. **New Company shares**: while the Company has Debt, the rightmost Stock Exchange piece moves into the Court (a Company share) and Debt
   drops by one. Then the remaining pieces slide right, keeping their order.

### III. Hiring (p.14-16)

Vacant offices are hired in ascending order of the number on their card's back, by the office and from the candidates printed there.

- **Nepotism**: hiring one's own family member needs the verbal consent of every other player in that candidate pool (not for the
  Chairman).
- **Promotions**: a promoted officeholder's old office goes vacant (in order); its fatigue moves to the new card.
- No candidate, or the hiring office vacant: the office stays vacant this turn.
- **Chairman**: anyone with a Company share may run; consent from players holding a majority of the shares (the former Chairman
  announces the number) elects at once. Else the former Chairman picks any shareholder, themselves included. The new Chairman moves a
  share into the Chairman slot (still a share, not tradable, not usable for firm investment). Empty Court: the former Chairman stays,
  with a piece from supply (or a prize), else no Chairman.
- **Director of Trade**: the Chairman hires any officeholder but the Chairman or a Governor (all vacant: any writer).
- **Manager of Shipping**: the Chairman hires any writer.
- **Military Affairs**: the Chairman hires a Commander in a Presidential Army; else any officer; else any Officer-in-Training.
- **Presidents**: the Director of Trade hires a writer or Governor of that Presidency.
- **Governors**: their President hires a writer or officer in the region (not a Commander); placed on the Governor overlay.
- **Superintendent of Trade in China**: the Chairman hires any writer (at once when the office is created).

### IV. Company Operation (p.16-26)

Each office acts in turn along the red ribbon, starting with the Chairman. Vacant offices are skipped. **No dead actions**: nothing that
would have no effect or no chance.

**Checks**: pick the resources to spend (paid to the bank from the office's treasury), take the dice they give, remove dice for the
penalty, roll; the lowest die decides: 1-2 success, 3-4 failure (may try again with more resources), 5-6 catastrophic failure (the
officeholder goes back to supply, the office goes vacant, no more actions).

- **Chairman**: may advance Debt up to 3 times (+£5 Balance each; more needs a majority of shares, the Chairman counting), then must
  allocate the whole Balance to the other offices' treasuries.
- **Director of Trade**: Special Envoy, any number of times (a check, £1 a die, no penalty; on success open trade with China, creating
  and hiring the Superintendent, or open one closed order in a region the Company doesn't control). Then up to two transfers of a
  writer or ship between Presidencies or sea zones.
- **Manager of Shipping**: must spend until the treasury holds £2 or less: lease an extra ship (£2), fit a ship from a shipyard (£3, no
  owner's consent needed), or buy a Company ship (£5, only with no ships left on shipyards). Ships go to any of the three sea zones.
- **Military Affairs**: up to two transfers of an officer or regiment between Armies; must assign every Officer-in-Training to an Army;
  then in each Army where another player has more pieces than the Commander's player, or the slot is empty, appoints a new Commander
  from a player with the most pieces there (the old Commander becomes an officer).
- **Presidency operations** (Bombay, Madras, Bengal): the President decides the order of itself, its Commander and its Governors (each
  finishing before the next; they must be offered their turn). Trade may come before or after any of them. A vacant President: the
  Chairman orders them, with no Trade and no alliance approval.
  - **Trade** (once per turn successfully): declare regions (the home region first, only regions reachable with the ships and open
    orders), check (£1 a die, minus 1 per declared region beyond home). On success, one writer per ship in the sea zone on open empty
    orders of declared regions, starting at the home port, each next to one already placed (filled order tokens once writers run out);
    never in another President's home region. Each order adds its value to the Company Balance; the President takes £1 per order
    filled; each player £1 per writer of theirs placed. Writers next to another Presidency's get a divider.
  - **Commander**: may buy the local alliances in its Army box with the President's money; then Deploy any number of times: target the
    home region, a region of its Presidency or one bordering it (not another President's home region or another Presidency's Company
    region; a Company region only with a closed order or unrest); check with a die per exhausted officer or regiment and the alliance's
    dice, minus the target's strength (its whole empire's). Each exhausted officer then rolls: a 6 sends it home. On success: loot (£4
    per tower level plus the face-up loot token, at least the surviving officers + 1) paid out £1 at a time, the Commander's share and
    officers first, then clockwise per officer, then £1 to the bank per regiment and alliance, round after round; trophies (one per
    tower level); tower and dome removed (a capital's flags too); orders opened and unrest removed; if not yet Company-controlled, the
    loot/control token goes above the President's box, the Governor overlay goes on, its Governor card goes to Vacant Offices. A
    catastrophic Deploy loses half the Commander's trophies (rounded up) and the Commander.
  - **Governor**: a shrinking dice pool (the number on its card); Administer repeatedly: a check with the dice left, no penalty; on
    success £1 (£2 after a failure) and build a Company ship (two successes: in the region, then to the sea zone), commission a regiment,
    or tax (£2 to the Balance or the President's treasury; unrest from the second tax a turn); on failure, unrest. The pool drops by 1
    each time; stop at a catastrophe, an empty pool, or at will.
- **Superintendent of Trade in China** (after Bengal): its treasury holds only ships (sent like a sea zone, counting as East). Trade in
  China once: a die per ship, needs export icons in Company regions; success adds £4 per export icon to the Balance and £1 each to the
  Superintendent.
- **Governor General** (a law): all Governors retire, overlays and half-built ships removed, no new Governors; replaces the Director of
  Trade (card, treasury, place on the ribbon), hired the same way, hires the Presidents. Each turn: +£3 Balance per Company region; then
  Govern (once successfully): £1 a die; each 6 puts unrest in every Company region; success gives £1 per Company region and, per Company
  region, a Company ship, a regiment, or a tax (£2 to the Balance or another treasury, plus unrest in a different region each time);
  failure puts unrest in every Company region.

### V. Bonuses (p.27)

£1 per shipyard with a fitted ship and per workshop.

### VI. Revenue (p.27)

Standing moving to its leftmost space ends the game at once (Company failure, Final Scoring).

1. **Expenses**: £1 per Debt, per regiment and officer in an Army (not Commanders), per ship in the three sea zones. Emergency loans
   (+1 Debt, +£5 each; past the track's end still +£5) cover a shortfall; then the Balance is set to 0. 1-2 emergency loans: Standing -1;
   3+: -2.
2. **Expectations**: Balance below the Expectations under the Standing: Standing -1. **Reading:** checked on the Balance after expenses,
   before dividends, as listed.
3. **Dividends**: the Chairman may pay any number; each costs £1 per share and pays £1 per share to its owner. More paid than
   Expectations: Standing +1.

### VII. Events in India (p.28-33)

Roll the storm die: a sea zone face storms that zone's player-owned ships (1-2 nothing, 3-4 fatigued, or sunk if already; 5-6 sunk, back
to its shipyard unfatigued); the three-storm face storms every zone. Then flip as many event tiles as the die shows. An event happens in
the region on the back of the next tile of the stack (Crisis and Peace at the Elephant). Then, after some events, the Elephant marches.

- **Writer loss**: a writer on an order that closes goes home; a filled order token flips to closed.
- **Cascade**: closing an order in a region with none open closes every open order connected to that region's orders (or the
  northernmost open order of a region whose connected order is already closed; a fully closed one cascades again). A region overflows
  once per tile but may be flooded several times.
- **Events**: Turmoil (close the northernmost open order, or Cascade), Windfall (£ to writers there and next door), Shuffle, Leader
  (strengthen if sovereign, Rebellion if dominated), Peace (at the Elephant; fully in a Company region it clears unrest and opens every
  order), Crisis (+1 ... at the Elephant), Foreign Invasion.
- **Crisis**: Invasion by a sovereign attacker: attacker's empire strength + the tile's modifier must exceed the defender's empire
  strength; success moves the defender to the attacker's empire (a defending capital's empire shatters; an attacker with no flag takes
  a starred one if any is left); failure costs the attacker a tower level. Rebellion by a dominated attacker against its sovereign only:
  success makes it sovereign (flag returned, all its orders close, or Cascade); failure costs the capital a level.
- **Attacks on the Company**: the primary Crisis (with +1 per unrest there), then a Rebellion of strength = its unrest in every other
  Company region with unrest; Commanders from Bombay downwards, each in their regions; a vacant Commander is played by Military Affairs,
  else the Chairman (no trophies either way). The Commander exhausts Army pieces equal to the strength (as many as possible); short of
  it, the region is lost. Held: unrest removed, a trophy to the Commander.
- **Region loss**: the Commander loses half its trophies (rounded up) and goes home; each officer in that Army rolls, home on a 6; the
  Governor goes home (card to unused); overlay, Company ship and unrest removed, a dome with one level back (a flag if it fell to an
  Invasion), its control token back loot side down (no more loot); after an Invasion Crisis all its orders close (or Cascade). Standing
  -1 for the first region lost this turn, -2 for the second, and so on; failure ends the game after this phase.
- **Foreign Invasion**: roll the storm die; an Invasion (strength = a die roll) in Bombay, Madras or Bengal for each storm zone (all
  three on the three-storm face; the next tile's region with none). Success closes every order (or Cascade), removes its flags (a
  capital's whole set), puts a Company region's dome back, and sets the strength to half the roll, rounded down.
- **Elephant's March**: after a successful Invasion, to the winning capital (Imperial Ambitions); else to the region on the top of the
  stack: in a Company region, its centre; in a dominated region, facing its sovereign; in a sovereign region, on the border matching the
  tile's shape (circle, triangle, square), turning clockwise past regions it already dominates (all dominated: the redirect arrow's
  shape, towards the capital). An invaded region holding the Elephant facing elsewhere gets it in its centre (it now rebels).

### VIII. Parliament Meets (p.34-35)

1. The Prime Minister reveals law cards one at a time, up to three, discussing each, and picks one (a dilemma must be taken at once);
   the rest are discarded.
2. Policy: the PM turns the dial's arm either way to the first space matching the law's icon. Each space is a consequence and a target
   (an enterprise type, Company shares or a prize): **Tax** (£1 per target owned, or the target returned to supply, the Chairman too;
   fitted ships of returned shipyards stay in play), **Bonus** (£1 per target), **Power** (swap the target's power marker with the next
   higher; at the top, swap any two others).
3. The PM reads the law and sets its starting support on the Vote track; debate.
4. **Voting**, from the PM clockwise, round after round until the PM stops (or a round with no votes): £1 from the family treasury = 1
   vote; each card's vote icons (turned when used, once per vote). Votes push the marker either way, no limit. Voting against joins the
   **opposition** for the rest of the vote (a supporter may switch to it later). The opposition's biggest voter is the **Opposition
   Leader** (ties: the holder keeps it); others may cast their votes on an opponent's behalf with consent. The PM can't vote against or
   lend enterprises against (except through the "Vote with my enterprises..." promise).
5. **Result**: -1 or less fails: the law is discarded (a dilemma's "If failed" resolves), the Opposition Leader becomes Prime Minister and
   moves the policy one space either way and resolves it. Otherwise it passes: it goes by the board, the PM takes a passed law token, the
   policy resolves, then the law's effect. A law that makes the Company fail ends the game after the vote.

**Deregulation vote** (1758, Long 1710): with Standing and/or Debt on a lined space the PM may call a special session before the London
Season (must, on a star). Passed: Debt halved (one space per two), Standing to "S", a passed law token for the PM, Deregulation in play.
Failed: the Company share VP token is placed or flipped in the Court (shares worth at most ±3); the PM stays and keeps the law; the PM
may vote against it.

### IX. Upkeep and Refresh (p.36)

Each family member on a prize costs its upkeep; unpaid, it goes home and its VP is lost (players may lend each other money). Then, unless
it was the last turn: writers back to their Presidency, filled order tokens and dividers off, extra ships back, exhausted officers and
regiments ready, active local alliances exhausted, turn marker on.

## Deregulation and firms (p.37-41)

In play in 1813, and once voted in 1758 or Long 1710. A **firm** is a family flipped to its firm charter, with a London treasury
(investments, expenses, fitting ships) and an India treasury (trade revenue, dividends, expenses; the rest moves to London). Family cash
can't go in or out of firm treasuries.

- **Firms phase** (after Family, before Hiring): **Firm Investments**, in no order until no one wants more (timing between firms: the
  Prime Minister decides): form a firm (return a Company share or flip a workshop to invested; one family member as its first share;
  value cube on the leftmost space; £5 to London); invest with the manager's consent (the same price, £5 and a share; max 10 shares);
  fit ships on shipyards for £3 with the owner's consent, or buy back a player's Company ship (£3, £2 fatigued, to the Shipping
  treasury); hostile takeover with a majority of its shares; negotiated mergers (all shareholders' consent; the highest-value firm
  stays). Weak Company shares (Standing or Debt on a lined space) and shares created this turn can't be used.
- **Firm Strategy**: every manager secretly picks a sea zone and home port (a strategy card) and the cash for its trade attempt (covered,
  then revealed; the card stays hidden), or Hobnob (no trade, no dividends, keeps its last dividend for special retirements).
- **Trade**: at the start of that Presidency's operations the firm reveals and rolls (a die per £); with a success its initiative is its
  ships minus its successes; the President's comes from its Trade roll. Lowest initiative fills first (ties: the President; vacant or no
  trade: the Chairman, after the Commander and Governors). A firm fills up to ships + 1 orders from its home port along orders it fills;
  an order filled already this turn gives half (rounded down). Firm value +1 per £6 added to its India treasury.
- **Expenses and dividends** (start of Revenue, before the Company): £1 per ship, plus £1 per share or the firm's value, whichever is
  higher; unpaid, the firm dissolves (its shares to Debtor's Prison, -1 VP each; ships back to shipyards; invested workshops stay
  invested), unless saved by emergency investments (each lowers its value by 1) or an emergency merger. Dividends from India: £1 per share
  to its owner and £1 to the manager, per dividend; the total is recorded with the dividend cube.
- **Special retirement**: in the London Season, a firm shareholder may retire one more piece from supply, spending no more than the
  highest dividend a firm of theirs paid (not a firm with no dividend cube or on 0). Firm storms use the strategy's sea zone (Hobnob: none).
- **Final Scoring**: each firm share (and each Debtor's Prison piece) scores the firm's value, fractions dropped; then each shareholder
  may make one special retirement.

## Final Scoring (p.9)

The game ends at the scenario's last turn, or when the Company fails.

1. **Power award**: power from each enterprise and Company share (their marker's place on the Power track; the bottom two are worth 0),
   +1 per passed law token, +2 for the Prime Minister, blackmail cards and one spouse that show power, +1 per trophy. Most and second most
   power score the table's VP for the final turn; tied for most share the lower value and no second is given; tied for second get nothing.
2. **Court and workshops**: Company survived: each share scores the Court's value; failed: each share loses it, and each workshop scores 1.
3. **Final retirement** (survived): everyone rolls attrition and retires their pensioners (no prestige cards). **Failure** (failed): draw a
   Company failure card and resolve it.

Ties: most window icons, then Prime Minister order.

## One and two players: the Crown (p.42-44)

The Crown is an automated family with two colours' pieces (36), driven by the Crown Handbook and the 10 AI cards (climate, Presidency,
direction, card and region priorities). Players and the Crown trade favours with the 12 Crown promise cubes (split evenly); difficulty
Easy, Normal, Hard or Expert. Its rules are in the Solo Handbook, also in the print-and-play folder. **Reading:** left for after the
multiplayer game; it doubles as a ready-made bot for testing.
